\begin{abstract}
Cross-dataset conformal prediction can provide misleading battery
state-of-health (SOH) evidence when prediction-time information and
benchmark construction are unclear. We audit real CALCE, NASA, and
Oxford cycle-level records through 12 diagnostic analyses, treating the
benchmark as the measurement object. A same-protocol baseline reaches
94.8\% coverage with a cell-block confidence-interval lower bound of
94.2\%, but this result uses the most recent measured SOH. In
method-matched comparisons, removing that reference changes coverage by
as much as -60.4 percentage points; the leave-NASA contrast is -31.9
points. The as-used schema bundle changes leave-NASA coverage by -12.0
points and increases false acceptance by +100 points at SOH 0.90 (exact
p=9.54e-07). The bundle changes multiple columns and the
independent-control gate fails, blocking field-specific causal
attribution. A heuristic localized-residual comparator reaches nominal
point coverage on 3 of 7 tasks and recovers 1 standard point failure.
Conformal SOH coverage should therefore not be treated as deployment
reliability until recent-label availability, split integrity, controls,
cell-aware uncertainty, and threshold decisions are audited.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Lithium-ion battery state-of-health (SOH) uncertainty estimates are
often evaluated through cross-dataset benchmarks, where a model
calibrated on one collection is tested on another. In maintenance- and
reliability-facing uses, it is tempting to read those intervals as
evidence that threshold decisions remain trustworthy under protocol
shift. This paper argues that such a reading is not warranted unless the
benchmark itself is audited as a measurement instrument and the
information available at prediction time is stated explicitly.

This case study uses only real cycle-level SOH records from the Center
for Advanced Life Cycle Engineering (CALCE), the National Aeronautics
and Space Administration (NASA), and Oxford, bounding external validity
to these collections. It does not claim a new SOH estimator,
persistence-anchor superiority, or unrestricted cross-protocol transfer.
Instead, it asks a narrower reliability-engineering question: how much
of the apparent validity of conformal SOH evidence depends on access to
the most recent measured SOH, and what remains interpretable after
schema, protocol, and decision-level checks?

The main contributions of this case study are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  It defines a benchmark-integrity audit for conformal SOH evidence,
  treating the benchmark and its information set as the measurement
  object.
\item
  It separates a method-selection ablation from method-matched contrasts
  and shows that recent-SOH access is a load-bearing, task-dependent
  measurement condition.
\item
  It tests as-used schema-bundle sensitivity with multiplicity-adjusted
  intervals and an independently permuted-feature negative control;
  because the control gate fails, bundle effects are not promoted as a
  validated field-specific mechanism.
\item
  It reports a corrected leave-NASA (LNAS) schema-bundle
  false-acceptance increase of +100.0 pp at threshold 0.90 (exact
  paired-cell p = 9.54e-07, n = 1127) while retaining the bundled,
  noncausal interpretation.
\item
  It uses a localized-residual comparator only as a diagnostic envelope,
  thereby distinguishing empirical recovery from a formal
  covariate-shift guarantee.
\end{enumerate}

The intended takeaway is cautionary and operational: raw cross-dataset
conformal coverage is not battery SOH deployment evidence unless the
benchmark's prediction-time information, split construction, controls,
and decision consequences have been audited.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec-related-work} reviews related work on conformal
prediction, battery SOH uncertainty, and benchmark integrity.
Section~\ref{sec-methodology} defines the pre-specified audit design and
claim boundaries. Section~\ref{sec-results} reports evidence on
recent-label dependence, schema-bundle sensitivity, residual drift,
decision consequences, and localized calibration.
Section~\ref{sec-discussion} discusses implications and limitations, and
Section~\ref{sec-conclusion} gives concluding remarks.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec-related-work}

Conformal prediction provides finite-sample predictive-coverage
guarantees under exchangeability
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic, lei2018distributionfree}. Weighted conformal
variants extend this logic to covariate shift when the shift mechanism
can be estimated \citep{tibshirani2019covariate}. Conformalized quantile
regression calibrates quantile-regression estimates to retain
finite-sample coverage \citep{romano2019conformalized}, and adaptive
conformal inference addresses distribution shift over time
\citep{gibbs2021adaptive}. These guarantees do not by themselves certify
a reliability benchmark. If the information set includes recent labels,
dataset identity, or bookkeeping fields unavailable in the intended
decision setting, nominal coverage can describe the constructed
benchmark rather than the intended threshold-decision problem. The
localized-residual analysis used here is only a diagnostic inspired by
this literature, not an implementation of density-ratio weighted
conformal prediction.

Battery SOH and remaining useful life (RUL) studies increasingly report
probabilistic forecasts, predictive intervals, and prognostic
performance under uncertainty
\citep{saxena2010evaluating, he2011remaining, liu2020futurecapacity},
and real-application SOH estimation has its own mature methodological
literature \citep{berecibar2016critical}. A parallel transfer-learning
literature studies how SOH estimators adapt across batteries, operating
conditions, and laboratory or field domains
\citep{ye2021domainadversarial, vilsen2022field, shen2024review}. These
studies motivate cross-domain evaluation, but they usually treat the
benchmark schema as a prerequisite for model comparison rather than as
the primary object of reliability analysis.

The benchmark-integrity concern is also connected to broader
machine-learning reliability work on dataset documentation, shortcut
learning, and leakage
\citep{gebru2021datasheets, geirhos2020shortcut, kapoor2023leakage}.
Those literatures warn that apparent predictive performance can be
driven by unintended signals or by data-splitting and
feature-availability choices. In a battery SOH benchmark, the
corresponding risk is not merely a statistical nuisance: a
feature-schema artifact can be mistaken for protocol drift, or a
recent-label proxy can make a maintenance-facing forecast look reliable
under conditions that are unavailable at decision time.

This paper therefore occupies a narrower gap. It is not a new SOH
estimator and not a broad survey of battery transfer learning. It is a
measurement-validity case study: the unit of analysis is the reliability
of the benchmark evidence itself, especially how conformal interval
behavior changes across recent-label, split, schema-bundle,
residual-drift, and SOH-threshold diagnostics.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec-methodology}

The audit is organized around pre-specified diagnostic analyses rather
than ad hoc result selection. A scripted results table aggregates the
outputs used for the manuscript, and the reported values are taken from
that table. All active rows are real battery state-of-health (SOH)
records from CALCE, NASA, and Oxford
\citep{calce_battery_data, nasa_pcoe_battery_data, howey2017oxford}; no
synthetic records are included in the reported evidence. The case object
is therefore the benchmark evidence produced by these public cycle-level
datasets, not a proposed battery-management algorithm. Table
\ref{tbl-datasets} summarizes the 45-cell source evidence base of 13,876
cycle rows and the 13,831 rows eligible for a strictly prior recent-SOH
feature. The 12 completed diagnostic analyses are a same-protocol
coverage baseline, a recent-label ablation, a cross-protocol transfer
diagnostic, a residual-stress robustness screen, a host-timing
robustness screen, a negative-control check, a method-family robustness
comparison, a threshold decision-utility audit, a schema-cleaning audit,
a hard-regime audit, the schema-to-decision audit, and a
localized-residual comparator.

For cell \(i\) and recorded cycle \(t\), \texttt{prev\_soh} is
\(SOH_{i,t-1}\): the immediately preceding recorded same-cell
capacity-SOH after sorting by dataset, cell identifier, and cycle index.
The \(t-1\) measurement must occur before the prediction event at \(t\).
Each cell's first row has no such measurement and is excluded before
every split is constructed (45 rows in total); no same-row target fill
or other imputation is used. Thus recent-SOH is an oracle only in the
sense that it assumes a previous capacity measurement is actually
available at prediction time, not because it accesses \(SOH_{i,t}\).

The primary nominal coverage level is 90\%. The manuscript reports
coverage, width, dependence-aware uncertainty, threshold decision
utilities, schema diagnostics, and localized-residual comparator
outcomes from the scripted results table. The schema family contains
five pre-specified contrasts across seven tasks and seven endpoints (one
coverage endpoint plus six SOH thresholds), giving 245 tests. Coverage
and false-acceptance deltas in Tables \ref{tbl-schema-deltas} and
\ref{tbl-false-acceptance-deltas} use Bonferroni-adjusted, SE-based
normal intervals from 1000 cell-block resamples; the family-wise level
0.05 gives a per-test level near \(2\times10^{-4}\) and z \(\approx\)
3.7. Zero-SE boundaries use the exact paired-cell sign test. By
contrast, the Main 94.2\%--97.1\% interval and the lower bounds in Table
\ref{tbl-adaptive-envelope} are 2.5/97.5-percentile cell-block bootstrap
intervals, while Table \ref{tbl-decision-tradeoff} reports uncorrected
95\% cell-block normal intervals. The limited number of battery-cell
blocks makes extreme-tail normal widths uncertain; this is an additional
reason to treat coverage flags as sensitivity signals and require a
negative-control gate. The same data interface generates text, tables,
and captions to avoid manual transcription.

The nominal target is used as the evaluation criterion, while individual
conformal variants select their calibration quantiles strictly on a
disjoint validation split (never tuned on the test set). For the
same-protocol diagnostic baseline, the selected operating point uses
q-level 0.93. This yields a realized test coverage of 94.8\%; the benign
validation-to-test over-coverage is not treated as a new performance
claim, but as the baseline's `apparent reliability' before domain shift
or artifact removal, setting the reference for subsequent
measurement-validity checks relative to the 90\% coverage target.

Persistence-anchor behavior is used as a diagnostic probe because it
exposes recent-label and anchor dependence. It is not presented as a
deployable or superior method. Cross-protocol failures are interpreted
as validity evidence, not as a failed attempt to force a positive
transfer claim. The analysis therefore treats negative and heterogeneous
findings as informative: a failure after schema cleaning is evidence
consistent with residual protocol drift, while a small, directionally
heterogeneous, or control-inconsistent schema effect bounds the claim
rather than generalizing it. Residual drift is retained as a boundary
that is not fully separable from recent-label dependence in this
evidence base.

The audit examines four diagnostic dimensions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  recent-label / anchor dependence;
\item
  as-used schema-bundle sensitivity;
\item
  residual protocol drift after schema checks;
\item
  SOH serviceability-threshold decision risk under finite-cell evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The main same-protocol diagnostic baseline is the selected
persistence-anchor protocol Mondrian conformal predictor
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}. The diagnostic families include quantile
regression (QR), conformalized quantile regression (CQR)
\citep{romano2019conformalized}, and natural-gradient boosting (NGBoost)
\citep{duan2020ngboost}. For the no-recent-SOH ablation condition, the
selected diagnostic is a gradient-boosting domain-Mondrian conformal
variant, a group-conditional calibration construction suited to settings
where exchangeability across domains is not credible
\citep{barber2023beyond}; it is used only to quantify the dependence of
the benchmark on recent-label access and is not presented as a method
contribution. Because the calibration-selection rule, not the analyst,
chooses the per-condition operating point, the full-feature and
no-recent-SOH conditions can select different conformal families; the
resulting same-protocol coverage change therefore reflects the selection
rule's response to removing recent labels rather than a same-method
feature ablation, and the method-matched feature-isolation contrast is
supplied separately by the row-matched recent-label reference comparison
reported in Section \ref{sec-results}. Gradient-boosting CQR is used for
decision-utility diagnostics, schema comparisons, and the
localized-residual comparator. Training, validation, calibration, and
test indices are pairwise disjoint per task. The as-used bundle contains
\texttt{source\_row\_id}, \texttt{target\_cycle\_index}, and the
permuted control column, whereas the clean-original schema removes all
three; the bundle contrast therefore cannot isolate a
bookkeeping-specific effect. The independent control adds a fixed-seed
(seed 1033) permutation of \texttt{log\_cycle\_index} to the clean
schema and refits the complete pipeline on the same rows.

A decision-risk cell is defined relative to a capacity-SOH
serviceability threshold and a predicted SOH interval with lower bound L
and upper bound U for a test record with true SOH y. The record is
threshold-acceptable when its lower bound L is at or above the
threshold, threshold-unacceptable when its upper bound U is below the
threshold, and uncertain when the interval straddles the threshold. A
false-acceptance event is a threshold-acceptable record whose true SOH y
is below the threshold; a false-rejection event is a
threshold-unacceptable record whose true SOH y is at or above the
threshold. The reported false-acceptance rate divides false-acceptance
events by the number of actually threshold-unacceptable records, the
false-rejection rate divides false-rejection events by the number of
actually threshold-acceptable records, and the uncertain rate divides
uncertain records by all records; each rate carries a cell-block
bootstrap confidence interval that resamples battery cells with
replacement. These thresholds are capacity-based decision cutoffs, not
demonstrated electrochemical or thermal safety boundaries. To avoid
over-reading sparse cells, a small-denominator suppression rule flags
any decision cell whose denominator is below 5 as exploratory rather
than as a headline rate.

Schema-bundle sensitivity is adjudicated by a pre-specified rule applied
identically to every task. A task is flagged when the
as-used-minus-clean coverage interval excludes zero and the absolute
delta is at least 3 pp.~This flag is necessary but not sufficient for a
mechanistic claim: the independent permuted-feature control must remain
null across both its coverage contrast and all six false-acceptance
endpoints. The bundle's false-acceptance-rate delta is evaluated
separately with multiplicity adjustment or, for zero-standard-error
boundaries, an exact paired-cell sign test; cells below 5 effective
observations are exploratory. This hierarchy prevents a bundled
association from being relabeled as a bookkeeping mechanism, while still
allowing a corrected bundle-level decision association to be reported
without field-specific attribution.

Two dependence boundaries are explicit. First, the conformal calibration
objects are cycle-level residuals from longitudinal cell records, so the
usual exchangeability interpretation is conditional on the task
construction rather than a free guarantee for independent operating
histories. The uncertainty intervals therefore resample cells as blocks
and are read as dependence-aware diagnostics, but the present evidence
does not replace a fully cell-independent prospective forecasting
design. Second, recent-SOH is treated as an oracle recent label in the
main dependence probe: it may be a legitimate autoregressive measurement
in a laboratory setting where the previous capacity has just been
measured, but it is not automatically available in an online
battery-management setting. The audit therefore interprets recent-SOH
access as a measurement condition to be declared, not as leakage in
every possible deployment scenario.

The SOH label is capacity-normalized within the harmonized project data
interface, and Table \ref{tbl-datasets} reports the ranges after quality
assurance (QA). Because the source datasets differ in cycling protocol,
temperature range, and capacity measurement practice, the analysis
treats cross-dataset label comparability as part of the
benchmark-validity boundary. The current manuscript does not establish a
full label-harmonization audit over alternative denominators, smoothing
rules, or anomaly-removal policies; such sensitivity analysis would be
needed before attributing every cross-dataset coverage failure to
physical protocol drift or model behavior.

Because a standard conformal failure is incomplete without a
localization sensitivity check, the audit also evaluates a heuristic
localized-residual CQR comparator. Calibration and target features are
standardized by calibration means and standard deviations. For target
row \(x\), calibration score \(r_i\) receives Gaussian weight
\(w_i(x)=\exp\{-d_i(x)^2/(2h^2)\}+10^{-12}\), where \(d_i^2\) is the
mean squared standardized distance over the recorded common shift
features. The local radius is the weighted conformal quantile. Bandwidth
\(h\in\{0.25,0.50,1,2,4,\infty\}\) is selected only on validation data
by the predeclared coverage-first, then width, objective; test metrics
are not used. This is motivated by covariate-shift conformal work
\citep{tibshirani2019covariate}, but it does not estimate a density
ratio and carries no formal covariate-shift guarantee. Meeting the
envelope means that point coverage reaches nominal; a lower-bound
shortfall crosses only when the standard percentile lower bound is below
nominal and the localized lower bound is at or above it. The result is
not interpreted as method superiority.

\section{Results}\label{sec-results}

The engineering object in this case study is not a new SOH estimator but
the reliability evidence produced by a cross-dataset conformal SOH
benchmark. The decision question is how interval coverage and SOH
threshold decisions change across recent-label access, split
construction, feature-schema sensitivity, residual protocol drift, and
localized residual weighting diagnostics.

The evidence base is a set of diagnostic contrasts over the same
real-data benchmark splits. Task abbreviations use Main for the
same-protocol held-out task, leave-CALCE (LCAL), target-CALCE (TCAL),
leave-NASA (LNAS), target-NASA (TNAS), leave-Oxford (LOxf), and
target-Oxford (TOxf). Table \ref{tbl-evidence-map} reports the
argument-bearing contrasts needed to interpret reliability evidence;
ancillary timing and residual-stress screens are treated as robustness

\section{Discussion}\label{sec-discussion}

The audit changes the interpretation of cross-dataset conformal SOH
benchmarks. Coverage is conditional on the benchmark's measurement
validity and information set; it is not automatically a reliability or
serviceability result. Recent-label access can make a benchmark easier
than the intended forecasting problem. The as-used schema-bundle
contrast reveals an unstable evaluation design, but it jointly changes
multiple columns, and the endpoint-wide independent-control gate fails
in LCAL and TCAL. The corrected LNAS false-acceptance association is
therefore retained at the bundle level without attribution to
bookkeeping fields or promotion to a causal schema mechanism. Residual
drift, finite-cell variation, and label-definition differences remain
plausible contributors.

For reliability and battery-health practitioners, the engineering
implication is a pre-submission benchmark-integrity discipline. Analysts
should declare whether a recent measured SOH is available at prediction
time, demonstrate that training, validation, calibration, and test
indices are disjoint, use cell-aware uncertainty, test schema claims
against independent negative controls, and report threshold decisions
with their denominators. A localization comparator can test sensitivity,
but it cannot substitute for a valid conformal guarantee. Table
\ref{tbl-audit-protocol} condenses this discipline into the audit
protocol applied in this case study; each gate is pre-specified, and a
failure bounds the claim rather than terminating the analysis.

The recent-label finding is deliberately not stated as a monotone
benefit. Method-matched deltas are strongly negative in TCAL, LNAS,
TNAS, and LOxf, smaller but still negative in LCAL, near zero in Main,
and positive in TOxf. That reversal matters: recent-SOH is not simply a
universally helpful covariate; it changes the estimand and interacts
with the target task. The practical failure is therefore undeclared
information availability. A benchmark using an oracle recent label
cannot support a no-recent-label deployment claim without a separate
evaluation.

The localized-residual comparator sharpens the interpretation without
turning the study into a method-success paper. It changes empirical
coverage in several tasks and recovers exactly one standard
point-coverage failure, but other tasks remain below target. Because it
is not a density-ratio conformal method, its results show only that
calibration is sensitive to local residual composition; they do not
establish shift-robust coverage.

The main limitation is external breadth. The evidence base is limited to
CALCE, NASA, and Oxford, so the case-study claim is not that every
battery SOH benchmark has the same sensitivity. A Massachusetts
Institute of Technology (MIT)-Stanford-Toyota fast-charge dataset
\citep{severson2019datadriven} is excluded because its
lithium-iron-phosphate chemistry and fast-charge-to-failure protocol
would add chemistry and protocol confounding, and its records did not
pass the same preprocessing and schema-compatibility checks. Broader
modern and field datasets are needed for external validation.

Several design limitations follow directly from the audit framing.
Calibration residuals are cycle-level observations from longitudinal
cell histories; cell-block intervals quantify rate uncertainty but do
not create independent exchangeable histories. A stronger follow-up
should use cell-disjoint prospective splits, report row-weighted and
cell-macro coverage, and show worst-cell behavior near serviceability
thresholds. The article treats recent-SOH as an oracle recent label;
deployment-facing work should compare oracle, estimated, and unavailable
recent-SOH on identical rows. The capacity-based label is harmonized
only through the current preprocessing interface. Moreover, the original
frozen processed CSV could not be recovered byte-for-byte; the repaired
analyses were regenerated from the preserved raw holdings under Python
3.11.15 and scikit-learn 1.7.2. Although raw split membership was
reproduced, this provenance discontinuity limits exact numerical
replication of the earlier exploratory run and is one reason the present
conclusions rely only on the corrected rerun.

The serviceability-threshold language is intentional. The audited SOH
thresholds (0.70, 0.80) are illustrative capacity-SOH cutoffs used to
demonstrate the diagnostic quantities. Their use in maintenance,
warranty, or end-of-life decisions requires application-specific
validation; this manuscript does not establish them as electrochemical
safety boundaries or thermal-runaway risk thresholds. Additional field
data, prospective forecasting splits, and application-specific risk
models are needed before the audit can be treated as a general
battery-management validation protocol.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec-conclusion}

This case study shows why conformal battery SOH benchmark results should
be audited before being treated as reliability evidence. In the
corrected real-data analyses, recent-label access produces large but
task-dependent method-matched coverage changes. The as-used schema
bundle changes coverage in two tasks and increases LNAS false acceptance
at one corrected threshold, but it jointly changes multiple columns and
the endpoint-wide independent-control gate fails, blocking attribution
to bookkeeping fields.

The contribution is therefore a bounded measurement-validity warning:
conformal SOH intervals may be useful, but their serviceability
interpretation depends on the benchmark-integrity discipline developed
and discussed above. Future work should apply this audit to broader
field data and prospective forecasting settings.

The implication for reliability engineering is simple: coverage must not
be promoted to deployment reliability until the benchmark's information
set, split integrity, controls, and decision consequences survive the
same audit.
